# Content Doctrine — permanent creative standing orders

_This file is the channel owner's creative doctrine. It is injected into EVERY
content generation call, ahead of the auto-generated strategy. Unlike
`strategy.md` (rewritten daily by `strategy_loop.py`), this file is never
touched by automation — edit it by hand or by asking Claude._

---

## 1. Speak to ONE person (the ICP)

Every hook is written to ONE specific man, not an audience: **18–34, on a
self-improvement arc — gym, ambition, quiet grind. He feels behind, distracted,
softer than he wants to be. He wants structure, inner strength, and an edge.**
The more specifically a hook lands on HIM, the more it converts. Generic wisdom
("Stoic quote of the day") converts nobody.

## 2. Men respond to being QUANTIFIED

Rank, percentile, count, deadline — numbers make the stakes personal. Keep the
calm register and dignity (we challenge, never insult), and keep every word
YouTube-safe:

- RIGHT: "Most men lose this fight before 9am." / "99% never train this." /
  "One habit separates you from him." / "You've restarted 14 times."
- ALSO WORKS (feel-seen register): "It's not weakness. It's untrained." /
  "Nobody taught you this."
- WRONG: insult-framing ("you're pathetic"), hype-bro shouting, anything
  graphic or shaming — that gets suppressed and breaks the channel's voice.

## 3. Copy proven formats — don't reinvent

The algorithm already told everyone what works. Steal structures that are
already viral in the self-improvement/philosophy niche and adapt them to
Stoicism (that's how the `story` format was chosen). Never invent a format from
nothing when a proven one can be adapted.

## 4. Test NEW, then multiply the WINNER until it dies

Structurally different tests beat 1000 variations of the same idea. But the
moment a format/hook style shows strong signal in analytics, make MANY
variations of that winner and ride it until performance decays. Then find the
next. (The experiment agent + channel report exist to surface these winners —
check them before changing direction.)

## 5. Leave the viewer better than you found them

_Owner, 2026-08-13: "I want the content to be more helpful to people. Choose
stories that if you watched them you'd feel better about yourself, and make as
many people get help as possible."_

This outranks every other rule in this file. When a hook could be sharper by
making someone feel worse, it does not get sharper. The test for any script is
one question: **if a man watched this at 2am on a bad night, would he feel
better about himself, or smaller?** If smaller, rewrite it.

**Pick stories where someone GOT THROUGH IT.** Not just endured, not just died
nobly — came out the other side and was okay. The Stoics are full of both;
choose the ones that end in relief, not grimness. A story that proves it is
survivable helps. A story that proves life is hard does not.

**Give one thing he can actually use tonight.** Not "contemplate mortality" —
something with a shape: a sentence to say, a thing to put down, a question to
ask himself, one action before sleep. Advice with no handle is decoration.

**Absolve before you instruct.** Name the thing he is beating himself up about
and take the shame off it first — "that is not weakness, it is untrained" —
THEN give the practice. Instruction before absolution reads as a lecture and
lands as another failure on the pile.

**Widen the door.** Write for the ordinary version of the struggle, not the
extreme: the tired, the stuck, the lonely, the ashamed — not only the man
chasing a PR at 5am. More people are quietly having a hard week than are
optimising. Universal beats niche for reach AND for help; they point the same
way here.

**Never make Stoicism a substitute for help.** If a script drifts toward real
despair, the turn must move him TOWARD people — call someone, tell someone,
let someone in — never "endure it alone, that is what a strong man does".
Isolation dressed up as strength is the single most harmful thing this niche
does, and this channel does not do it.

**Banned outright:** shame as motivation, "nobody is coming to save you" as a
closer, contempt for people who are struggling, anything that treats needing
help as weakness. These get engagement and they hurt people. Not on this
channel.


## 6. Direct the picture. It is not wallpaper.

_Owner, 2026-08-25: "they're not creative enough... content that's just being
generated and has no substance for a human to even remotely feel attached to,
so they just scroll away. Think like a creative director."_

Proven by watching two real published videos: a melted AI marble bust while a
voice asks "who did you snap at?". The image and the words had no relationship
at all. That is wallpaper, and wallpaper is what people scroll past.

**Shot 1 must pose a question the picture cannot answer.** The script answers
it. A statue announces "philosophy video" in 200ms and the thumb moves. Calm
grey sea under the line "I'm not ill, and I'm not well" makes a viewer wait
three seconds to find out why the sea.

**Follow the script's OWN image, not its mood.** Serenus said sea-sickness
without a storm — so the shot is a swell with no storm, a glass of water
trembling, a rocking chair still moving in an empty room. Not "a sad man in a
dark bedroom", which is the mood and means nothing.

**Never show what the voice is saying.** If the narration says he lay awake
and the picture is a man lying awake, the viewer receives one idea twice and
learns nothing in the gap. Text and image should meet, not repeat.

**Specific beats atmospheric.** "A full glass of water trembling on a table"
is a shot. "A moody dark room" is a search query. If a clip could illustrate
any of the thirty scripts, it is illustrating none of them.

**The frame must be visible.** Measured on real output: 12.8-21.5% mean
luminance, which reads as a black rectangle at the phone brightness this
viewer uses at midnight. Mood is not the same as darkness.
